RITUAL TROPHIES
===============

Purpose
-------

Of Taming and Breeding normally follows a simple principle:

    If a creature can reasonably be tamed, the player should interact
    with that creature and gradually earn its trust.

This works naturally for animals and many other living creatures.

Valheim, however, contains several beings for which conventional taming
does not make much sense.

How would a Ghost be fed until it trusts the player?

How would a Skeleton be domesticated?

What would taming a Stone Golem through food even represent?

The same problem can occur with animated or otherwise unnatural beings such
as Kvastur.

These creatures exist, move, fight and can be destroyed, but they are not
necessarily living organisms in the ordinary sense.

Ritual Trophies were introduced to give such creatures an alternative path
into OTAB's taming system without pretending that every creature in Valheim
can simply be domesticated by throwing food at it.


THE BASIC CONCEPT
-----------------

A Ritual Trophy is a modified version of an existing creature trophy.

The original trophy remains an important component of the ritual.

In Valheim, trophies usually represent a recognizable physical remainder of
a defeated creature. Most commonly this is its head, although some creatures
are represented by another characteristic part, such as the Wraith Trophy
being represented by its cloak.

Vanilla Valheim already uses trophies for purposes beyond decoration.

They can function as ritual or progression items, such as Deer Trophies
being used to summon Eikthyr, and they can also be used by the Ballista to
identify specific targets.

OTAB extends this existing concept.

For a creature that does not appear to be conventionally alive, its trophy
provides a remaining physical connection to something that previously
existed.

OTAB deliberately does NOT define this connection as a captured soul.

The trophy may be a remnant, an anchor, a representation, or simply an
essential component of the ritual.

Exactly what remains of the creature and what allows such a being to exist
in the first place is intentionally left unanswered.


RESTORATION RATHER THAN DOMESTICATION
-------------------------------------

Ritual Trophies are not conventional taming.

The original creature cannot be fed until it becomes friendly. Instead, the
player first has to encounter and defeat the creature and obtain its trophy.

That trophy can then become the central component of a ritual intended to
bring such a being back into existence under the player's control.

What "bringing it back" actually means depends on the creature.

For a Ghost, it might resemble manifestation or restoration of a
supernatural existence.

For a Skeleton, it could resemble reconstruction or reanimation.

For a Stone Golem, biological resurrection would make little sense at all.
Its restoration may instead involve reconstructing and reanimating the
material from which such a being is formed.

An animated object such as Kvastur presents yet another case.

OTAB therefore deliberately avoids defining Ritual Trophies as a universal
form of necromancy.

The common principle is simply:

    Something existed.
    It was destroyed.
    Something of it remained.
    Through a ritual, that existence can be restored.

The exact supernatural process is left undefined.


THE TROPHY AS AN ANCHOR
-----------------------

The requirement for the original creature trophy is important both
thematically and mechanically.

A Ritual Trophy cannot normally be used as an easy way to bypass encountering
the original creature.

The player must first be capable of fighting the creature and must obtain its
trophy.

Additional ritual materials can then reflect the nature of the creature.

The same underlying Ritual Trophy system can therefore represent very
different kinds of restoration without requiring a single explanation for
all of them.

The trophy establishes the connection to the original creature.

The remaining ingredients provide whatever else the individual ritual
requires.


AN UNCERTAIN RITUAL
-------------------

Internally, Ritual Trophies use parts of OTAB's egg and growth system.

Conceptually, however, they are explicitly NOT eggs.

An egg represents a living creature developing over time.

A Ritual Trophy does not grow.

Because of this distinction, Ritual Trophies do not use a normal,
predictable growth duration.

After being placed in the world, the ritual may remain dormant for an
unknown amount of time before succeeding.

There is no biological progression from:

    young -> growing -> adult

and there is no meaningful:

    25% -> 50% -> 75% -> 100%

growth process.

Instead, the Ritual Trophy remains what it is until the ritual succeeds.

Conceptually:

    Ritual Trophy
          |
          | unknown duration
          |
          ?
          |
          v
    Manifestation / Restoration

The uncertain duration serves two purposes.

Mechanically, it strongly distinguishes Ritual Trophies from ordinary eggs.

Thematically, it avoids suggesting that a Ghost, Skeleton, Golem or animated
object is slowly growing inside a trophy.

Nothing is being born.

The creature simply returns to existence when the ritual succeeds.


CREATURE-SPECIFIC RESTORATION
-----------------------------

The Ritual Trophy is a framework rather than a rule that every creature must
return in exactly the same way.

A Ghost can manifest directly as a tamed Ghost.

A more physical creature can require additional stages.

The Stone Golem is an example of this distinction.

Its ritual initially produces a very small form of the Golem rather than an
immediately completed full-sized creature. That form subsequently develops
through additional stages until the reconstruction is complete.

This does not mean that the Golem is biologically growing like an animal.

It represents the restoration of a fundamentally different kind of being.

Other Ritual Trophy creatures may behave differently where their nature
requires it.

The ritual mechanic is shared.

The interpretation of the ritual is creature-specific.


LIMITATIONS AND PROGRESSION
---------------------------

Ritual Trophies are not intended as a method for turning powerful unnatural
creatures into easily renewable livestock.

The system contains several natural limitations.

First, the player needs the original creature's trophy.

A Ritual Trophy therefore remains connected to encounters with the wild
creature and to the player's progression through Valheim.

Second, creatures obtained through these rituals cannot reproduce.

Their existence is not based on biological reproduction, and allowing two
ritual creatures to produce offspring would undermine the reason the Ritual
Trophy system exists in the first place.

If another one is desired, another appropriate trophy and another ritual are
required.

Third, these creatures are not given artificial biological needs merely to
make them fit Valheim's normal taming mechanics.

A Ghost does not suddenly need ordinary food because it belongs to the
player.

This has an important gameplay consequence.

Valheim's normal tamed-creature healing is tied to feeding. A ritual creature
that cannot eat cannot simply be kept fed in order to regenerate like a
normal domesticated animal.

Damage to such a creature therefore matters.

The result is intentionally different from breeding ordinary livestock or
maintaining a pack of conventionally tamed animals.


THE RITUAL SYMBOL
-----------------

Ritual Trophies are visually distinguished from ordinary trophies by an
additional magical symbol.

Their inventory icons retain the appearance of the corresponding original
trophy, while the ritual symbol appears behind it.

The distinction also exists physically in the world.

When a Ritual Trophy is placed on the ground, the same symbol appears
beneath the trophy as an attached visual.

This makes the ritual state visible without replacing the identity of the
original trophy.


AÐ STILLA ALLA REIÐI
--------------------

The symbol used for Ritual Trophies is based on a historically documented
Icelandic magical stave known as:

    Að stilla alla reiði

The phrase can approximately be understood as:

    "To still all anger"
    "To calm all anger"

The historical stave was associated with calming anger and conflict.

It was NOT historically a symbol for resurrection, summoning the dead,
creating creatures, or taming animals.

OTAB does not claim otherwise.

Its historical meaning was one of the reasons the symbol became especially
appropriate for Ritual Trophies.

The ritual itself represents the restoration or manifestation of the
creature.

The symbol can instead represent another important part of the process:

    the pacification of what returns.

The creature does not return as another hostile wild creature. It returns
already bound to, or at peace with, the player.

OTAB therefore uses the historical meaning symbolically rather than
redefining it as a fictional resurrection rune.


HISTORICAL CONTEXT
------------------

Að stilla alla reiði belongs to the broader tradition generally associated
with Icelandic magical staves, or galdrastafir.

These symbols should not be confused with Viking Age runes.

The surviving Icelandic magical manuscripts containing such staves are
considerably later than the Viking Age and reflect a mixture of traditions,
including both pre-Christian Nordic elements and Christian influences.

This distinction is important.

OTAB does not present the Ritual Trophy symbol as an authentic Viking Age
rune.

Instead, it uses historical Nordic/Icelandic magical imagery in much the same
spirit that Valheim itself freely combines mythology, folklore and historical
material from different periods into its fantasy setting.

Valheim provides a particularly relevant precedent through the Vegvísir,
another Icelandic magical stave which the game reinterprets as a tool for
revealing the locations of Forsaken altars.


DESIGN ORIGIN
-------------

The visual form of Að stilla alla reiði first came to the author's attention
through material surrounding Senua's Saga: Hellblade II.

This was the initial inspiration to investigate the symbol rather than the
source from which OTAB copied its artwork.

Að stilla alla reiði is a historically documented magical stave independent
of Hellblade.

OTAB uses its own visual implementation of the historical motif and connects
it to the Ritual Trophy system because the stave's documented association
with calming anger provides an unusually fitting symbolic meaning.

The distinction is important:

    Hellblade II drew attention to the symbol.

    Historical galdrastafir provided the source and context.

    OTAB gives the symbol its own role within its taming system.


WHAT OTAB DOES NOT DEFINE
-------------------------

The Ritual Trophy system intentionally leaves several questions unanswered.

OTAB does not establish that:

    trophies contain souls;

    every ritual is a form of necromancy;

    every ritual creature is literally resurrected;

    Að stilla alla reiði historically summoned or resurrected creatures;

    all supernatural creatures in Valheim exist through the same process;

    the player understands exactly what happens during the ritual.

These gaps are intentional.

Valheim frequently leaves the exact nature of its supernatural world open to
interpretation, and OTAB does not need to construct a complete magical system
in order to explain a taming mechanic.

The Ritual Trophy provides enough context for the mechanic to make sense
without attempting to answer questions that Valheim itself leaves open.


SUMMARY
-------

Ritual Trophies exist for creatures that do not reasonably fit conventional
taming.

Instead of making every creature eat arbitrary food, OTAB gives these beings
a separate acquisition path based around their existing trophies.

The player must first encounter the creature, defeat it and obtain a
meaningful remnant of its previous existence.

That remnant becomes the anchor of an uncertain ritual.

If the ritual succeeds, the creature returns in a tamed state.

How that restoration works is deliberately dependent on the creature and is
not universally defined.

Ritual creatures do not reproduce, and creatures incapable of eating do not
gain artificial diets merely because they have become tamed.

The system therefore follows a simple principle:

    Not everything can be tamed.

    Some things can only be brought back.

And when they return, Að stilla alla reiði represents not the act of
resurrection itself, but the calming of what has returned.
