OTAB STANDARD DATA PACK
=======================

About This Data Pack
--------------------

This is the standard data pack distributed with Of Taming and Breeding.

It is intended as the recommended configuration for players who want a
mostly vanilla-friendly Valheim experience with additional taming, breeding
and creature interactions.

The goal is not to make Valheim completely different.

The goal is also not to reproduce Vanilla exactly.

Instead, this data pack tries to expand the possibilities available to the
player while respecting the identity, lore and progression of Valheim as
much as reasonably possible.

It can be used as-is as a plug-and-play configuration, but none of its
settings are mandatory.

The data files are intentionally configurable and may be changed by players
and server administrators.


VANILLA-FRIENDLY DOES NOT MEAN VANILLA
--------------------------------------

Any mod that changes which creatures can be tamed, how they behave, how they
reproduce, or what the player can do with them inevitably changes Valheim's
gameplay and progression.

This data pack is no exception.

It should therefore not be understood as:

    "Vanilla, but with no balance changes."

That would be impossible.

Instead, "vanilla-friendly" describes the design direction.

Whenever possible, the configuration starts with what Valheim itself tells
or shows us about a creature.

This includes things such as:

    - Lore and runestones
    - Spawn locations and biomes
    - Existing behavior
    - Diet and available food
    - Factions and relationships
    - Drops and trophies
    - Appearance
    - Environmental storytelling
    - Relative strength and rarity
    - Existing progression
    - Other observable Vanilla mechanics

These details are used to understand what makes a creature distinct before
deciding how OTAB should interact with it.

This does not mean that every resulting decision represents the intentions
of Valheim's developers.

Where no explicit answer exists, the data pack may make its own
interpretation.

Such interpretations are exactly that:

    interpretations.

They are attempts to build upon Valheim's existing world rather than claims
about undocumented Vanilla lore or developer intent.


VANILLA IS THE STARTING POINT, NOT A PRISON
-------------------------------------------

Strict adherence to Vanilla would leave very little reason for this data
pack to exist.

OTAB adds possibilities that Vanilla does not provide, and the standard data
pack makes use of them.

The important distinction is how those additions are approached.

Existing lore and mechanics are considered first.

Where Valheim leaves gaps, the data pack may connect existing information,
extend an idea, or introduce a limited new interpretation if doing so creates
an interesting mechanic without unnecessarily replacing the creature's
original identity.

Some decisions therefore have strong direct support from Vanilla.

Others are extrapolations from environmental storytelling or existing
mechanics.

Some are deliberately new OTAB interpretations.

Where a distinction is important, accompanying design and lore notes should
make it clear which is which.

The objective is not:

    "Would Vanilla definitely do this?"

The better question is:

    "Can this exist alongside what Vanilla has already established?"


NOT EVERYTHING SHOULD BE TAMEABLE
---------------------------------

The existence of a taming system does not mean that every creature should be
tameable.

This is one of the central principles behind the standard data pack.

Before deciding how a creature should be tamed, the first question is:

    Should this creature be tameable at all?

Sometimes the answer is no.

Bosses, for example, are not treated as pets or ordinary tameable creatures.

Other creatures may remain unavailable because their identity, lore,
behavior or place in the game provides no convincing reason to make them
obtainable.

The standard data pack does not attempt to find an excuse to make every
creature tameable.

This is intentional.

The underlying OTAB systems may allow players or server administrators to
create configurations far beyond the decisions made here.

That flexibility does not mean that the standard data pack has to use every
possibility.


CREATURE IDENTITY COMES FIRST
-----------------------------

Making a creature tameable should not erase what made that creature
interesting in the first place.

A tamed Wolf is still a Wolf.

A tamed Troll is still a Troll.

A supernatural being does not suddenly become an ordinary animal simply
because the game considers it tamed.

For this reason, "tamed" should primarily be understood as a gameplay state,
not as a new universal creature identity.

In some cases, taming represents genuine domestication or trust.

In others, the creature may merely accept the player.

It may tolerate the player because the player provides something it wants.

It may cooperate only under certain conditions.

A supernatural creature may be bound or pacified rather than domesticated
in any biological sense.

These distinctions can remain visible after taming.

The standard data pack may use differences in:

    - Food
    - Feeding behavior
    - Taming method
    - Taming duration
    - Commandability
    - Growth
    - Procreation
    - Creature relationships
    - Group and faction behavior
    - Behavior while fed or hungry

to preserve or further define the identity of individual creatures.

In particular, OTAB's creature relationship rules allow a tamed creature to
retain meaningful relationships with players, its own group, other
factions, wild creatures and other tamed creatures.

Becoming tamed does not have to mean becoming a generic friendly pet.

A useful principle throughout this data pack is therefore:

    Tamed is a state, not a new creature identity.


TAMING DOES NOT ALWAYS MEAN FEEDING
-----------------------------------

Conventional taming works well for many creatures.

The player provides suitable food, the creature gradually becomes accustomed
to the player, and eventually it becomes tame.

For other creatures this would be difficult to justify.

Some beings in Valheim are undead, spectral, constructed, animated or
otherwise not conventionally alive.

Giving every one of them an arbitrary food item merely so that the normal
taming system can be used would make the system mechanically consistent, but
the creatures themselves less distinct.

The standard data pack may therefore use completely different acquisition
methods where appropriate.

Ritual Trophies are one example.

They provide an alternative for certain beings that cannot reasonably be
fed or conventionally domesticated.

Other creatures may simply remain untameable.

The method should follow the creature rather than forcing every creature to
follow the same method.


PROGRESSION WITHOUT HARD GATES
------------------------------

The standard data pack deliberately avoids using global progression keys as
mandatory gates for taming.

Valheim itself often allows skilled, prepared or adventurous players to
enter dangerous areas earlier than expected.

A player can, for example, encounter and attempt to tame a Wolf before
following the usual biome progression.

That freedom is part of the game.

The standard data pack tries not to remove it simply to enforce one intended
order.

Instead, progression restrictions should preferably emerge naturally from
the requirements of the creature.

A creature may require resources that are normally obtained in a later
biome.

It may be dangerous enough that attempting to tame it early is already a
significant challenge.

Its preferred food may itself require the player to take risks beyond their
current progression.

A Troll is one example of this approach.

The creature can theoretically be approached early, but its meaningful food
includes Entrails, normally obtained from the Swamps.

A player who manages to obtain the necessary resources early is not
artificially prevented from using them.

This creates a soft progression boundary rather than an absolute one.

OTAB still provides systems that can be used to impose stricter progression
requirements.

Server administrators and alternative data packs are free to use them.

The standard data pack simply chooses not to make hard progression gates its
default philosophy.


PROCREATION IS NOT AUTOMATIC
----------------------------

A creature being tameable does not automatically mean that it should be able
to reproduce.

Breeding is considered separately for each creature.

Biology, lore, gameplay implications and the creature's existing role all
matter.

Some creatures can reasonably reproduce.

Some may produce offspring that are not identical to themselves.

Others have no appropriate biological reproduction at all.

For supernatural, undead or constructed beings, ordinary breeding may be
completely inappropriate.

The standard data pack therefore does not treat procreation as a reward that
every tameable creature automatically receives.

As with taming itself, the question is not simply whether OTAB can enable a
mechanic.

The question is whether enabling it makes sense for that creature.


INSPIRATION
-----------

Of Taming and Breeding was inspired in part by AllTameable Taming Overhaul.

AllTameable is an extensive taming mod with its own ideas and systems,
including features such as its DNA system.

The standard OTAB data pack is not intended as a statement that this approach
is wrong.

It follows a different design preference.

A universal taming system can make a very large range of creatures
obtainable, but personally I found that applying similar taming rules to
nearly everything could make individual creature identities less important.

OTAB's standard data pack therefore begins from a different question.

Instead of:

    "How can we make every creature tameable?"

it asks:

    "Should this creature be tameable?"

and, if the answer is yes:

    "What should taming this particular creature mean?"

Sometimes the answer is ordinary feeding and domestication.

Sometimes it is tolerance.

Sometimes it requires an entirely different system.

And sometimes the answer remains:

    It should not be tameable.


INTERPRETATION AND RESEARCH
---------------------------

Many decisions in this data pack involve subjects that Valheim does not
explicitly explain.

Rather than filling those gaps arbitrarily, the intention is to research the
available information first.

Lore texts, runestones, creature behavior, environmental details, spawn
locations, drops and other Vanilla information are used wherever they can
provide useful context.

Possible interpretations are then questioned against that information.

This process cannot reveal the undocumented intentions of Valheim's
developers.

It can only provide evidence for what appears consistent with the world they
created.

For that reason, speculation should remain identifiable as speculation.

A good idea does not become Vanilla lore simply because it fits.

At the same time, uncertainty does not mean that no new interpretation is
allowed.

The standard data pack deliberately occupies the space between those two
extremes:

    Respect what Vanilla establishes.

    Investigate what Vanilla implies.

    Be honest about what OTAB adds.


A RECOMMENDED CONFIGURATION, NOT A RULESET
------------------------------------------

The standard data pack represents one curated configuration.

It is intended for players who want to install OTAB, use the supplied data,
and have a reasonably coherent vanilla-friendly experience without designing
every creature configuration themselves.

In that sense, it is the recommended plug-and-play configuration.

It is not the only correct way to configure OTAB.

The data files are deliberately accessible and configurable.

Players and server administrators are encouraged to change them if their
preferred experience differs from the assumptions made here.

A hardcore server may impose much stricter progression requirements.

Another server may prefer more aggressive creature relationships.

Some players may want easier taming or breeding.

Others may want something deliberately chaotic and completely unconcerned
with lore or balance.

Those are all valid uses of the configurable data system.

This standard pack simply provides a common starting point.


BALANCE AND CALIBRATION
-----------------------

The initial values in this data pack are personally calibrated estimates.

Taming times, food requirements, feeding durations, breeding behavior,
creature relationships and other balance decisions have been chosen based
on what currently appears appropriate for regular play.

They should not be considered final simply because they are included as the
default configuration.

Public testing and player feedback can reveal problems that theoretical
balancing cannot.

The standard data pack is therefore expected to be refined through testing,
especially where a configuration turns out to be substantially stronger,
weaker, easier, slower or more exploitable than intended.

Even after that process, its values remain recommendations rather than
immutable rules.

The purpose of the standard data pack is ultimately simple:

    Install it.
    Play with it.
    Change it if you want something different.


SUMMARY
-------

The standard OTAB data pack aims for:

    Vanilla-friendly, not Vanilla-identical.

    Expanded possibilities, not unrestricted access to everything.

    Soft progression where possible, rather than arbitrary hard gates.

    Creature-specific taming instead of one universal solution.

    Preserved creature identity after taming.

    Lore and observable Vanilla behavior as the starting point.

    Clearly separated interpretation where Vanilla provides no answer.

    Configurable data rather than fixed rules.

Most importantly:

    Not everything needs to be tameable.

    Not everything tameable needs to breed.

    Not everything tamed needs to behave like a pet.

The purpose of this data pack is not to make every creature fit the taming
system.

It is to make the taming system fit the creatures.
