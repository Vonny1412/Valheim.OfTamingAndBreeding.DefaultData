GREYDWARF FAMILY
================

Purpose
-------

This document records the design and lore reasoning behind OTAB's handling
of the Greydwarf family:

    Greyling
    Greydwarf
    Greydwarf Brute
    Greydwarf Shaman

All four creatures can be tamed in OTAB, but they cannot reproduce.

This decision is based on their established origin in Valheim and on a small
extension of the environmental lore surrounding the Black Forest.


VANILLA LORE
------------

Greydwarfs have an unusually detailed origin compared to many other
creatures in Valheim.

A Black Forest runestone describes them as "the soulless ones" and explains
that they originate when the soul of a murderer or great sinner rots beneath
the ground.

A hollow growth forms around it and attracts materials from the surrounding
forest. Wood, stone, moss, peat, reeds and other materials eventually form
the body of the creature.

The same runestone describes them as having:

    "nothing on their tongues or behind their eyes"

Although a human soul begins this process, the resulting creature is
therefore not treated as the original human simply returning from death.

Another runestone describes the Greydwarfs as being raised by the
"Old Man of the Forest" from "seeds of sin".

They take forms resembling the humans they once were, but no longer possess
their former human warmth. Their purpose is instead connected to serving the
Elder and protecting the forest.

Greydwarfs should therefore not be understood as ordinary animals.

They are supernatural forest creatures created from the consequences of
human death and sin, combined with the material of the forest itself.


THE GREYDWARF FAMILY
--------------------

OTAB treats Greyling, Greydwarf, Greydwarf Brute and Greydwarf Shaman as
members of the same general family.

There is strong support for the close relationship between Greydwarfs,
Brutes and Shamans: Greydwarf Nests can produce all three forms.

The exact relationship of the Greyling to the other forms is less clearly
explained by Valheim.

Its appearance and behavior strongly associate it with the Greydwarf family,
but Valheim does not explicitly state that a Greyling grows into a Greydwarf
or represents a younger Greydwarf.

OTAB therefore does not define a specific lifecycle for these creatures.

Likewise, there is no confirmed explanation for why one "seed of sin"
becomes an ordinary Greydwarf while another becomes a Brute or Shaman.

Possible explanations, such as the nature of the original person influencing
the resulting form, remain speculation and are not treated as established
Valheim lore by OTAB.


WHY THEY CAN BE TAMED
---------------------

Greydwarfs are supernatural creatures, but nothing in their known lore
requires them to be permanently incapable of forming another allegiance.

They are numerous creatures rather than unique entities, bosses or
independent higher beings.

Their connection to the Elder and the forest remains an important part of
their identity. Taming them in OTAB is therefore not intended to turn them
into ordinary domesticated animals or to change their supernatural origin.

Instead, OTAB assumes that an existing Greydwarf can gradually be made to
accept and follow the player through feeding.

This does not change how Greydwarfs are created and does not imply that the
human being from which one originated has somehow been restored or redeemed.

The creature remains a Greydwarf.


WHY THEY CAN BE COMMANDED
-------------------------

Valheim describes Greydwarfs as soulless beings with nothing on their
tongues or behind their eyes, created to serve the Elder.

OTAB interprets this servitude as part of their very nature, rather than
as a conscious choice. A Shaman heals and a Brute protects because these
are the roles they were given.

Taming may change whom a Greydwarf serves, but not its need to serve.
The Elder is replaced by the player; the purpose remains.

Perhaps that purpose is what keeps them moving. Perhaps it is also what
torments them: even freed from the Elder, they are never truly free.

This is an OTAB interpretation, not established Valheim lore.


FED AND WILD GREYDWARFS
-----------------------

Taming does not completely sever a Greydwarf's connection to its own kind.

While fed, a tamed Greydwarf is willing to fight wild members of the
Greydwarf family on the player's behalf.

Once it is no longer fed, it remains tamed and friendly towards the player,
but will no longer fight wild Greydwarfs.

This reflects the distinction between changing a creature's allegiance and
changing what the creature is.


YELLOW MUSHROOMS
----------------

The Greydwarf family can be tamed and fed with Yellow Mushrooms.

Valheim does not state that Greydwarfs eat Yellow Mushrooms, nor does it
explain why Yellow Mushrooms have their unusual appearance.

Their placement in the world, however, provides the basis for OTAB's
interpretation.

Yellow Mushrooms are strongly associated with crypts and other underground
places dominated by death and decay, including Burial Chambers and Sunken
Crypts.

OTAB interprets this as a sign that these mushrooms are somehow nourished or
changed by the unusual environment found in such places.

This is deliberately left undefined.

OTAB does NOT assume that Yellow Mushrooms contain souls or directly absorb
the souls of the dead. They may instead absorb some unknown byproduct of
death, decay, supernatural activity, or the environment created by these
things.

This makes them thematically appropriate food for Greydwarfs.

Greydwarfs themselves originate through a process involving death, a
decaying sinful soul, the soil and organic material. Valheim's own lore even
compares their appearance from the ground to mushrooms springing from the
soil.

OTAB therefore assumes that whatever makes Yellow Mushrooms thrive in places
associated with death and decay is also something that attracts or nourishes
Greydwarfs.

This connection is an OTAB interpretation and is not established Valheim
canon.


WHY THEY DO NOT PROCREATE
-------------------------

None of the Greydwarf forms can reproduce through OTAB's breeding system.

This follows directly from their established lore.

Greydwarfs are not described as being born from other Greydwarfs. Their
origin is instead tied to sinful human souls, the ground, material from the
forest and the influence of the Elder.

Allowing two tamed Greydwarfs to produce offspring would replace one of the
clearest creature origins given by Valheim with ordinary animal breeding.

OTAB therefore leaves this part of their identity unchanged.

Taming an existing Greydwarf is possible.

Creating new Greydwarfs through breeding is not.


THE BLACK FOREST CYCLE
----------------------

There is an additional connection between Troll and Greydwarf lore which
OTAB considers an interesting possible interpretation of the Black Forest.

Valheim describes Trolls as creatures that gnaw on the bones of the weak
and foolhardy.

Separately, Valheim states that the soul of a murderer or great sinner can
rot beneath the ground and eventually become the origin of a Greydwarf.

Valheim does NOT explicitly connect these two pieces of lore.

OTAB nevertheless considers them compatible parts of the same environmental
cycle.

A sinful or violent person could die in the Black Forest, possibly even as
the victim of a Troll. The body becomes food or eventually decays, while the
sinful soul enters the process described by the Greydwarf runestones.

In such a case:

    Human
      |
      | death in the Black Forest
      v
    Body / remains --------> consumed or left to decay
      |
      | sinful soul
      v
    "Seed of sin"
      |
      | forest / soil / Elder
      v
    Greydwarf

This is not presented as Valheim canon.

It is an OTAB interpretation connecting two existing pieces of Black Forest
lore without changing either of them.


SUMMARY
-------

    Creature             Tameable    Procreation
    ---------------------------------------------
    Greyling              Yes         No
    Greydwarf             Yes         No
    Greydwarf Brute       Yes         No
    Greydwarf Shaman      Yes         No

The central distinction is:

    Taming changes a Greydwarf's relationship with the player.
    It does not change what a Greydwarf is or how Greydwarfs are created.

Their supernatural origin, connection to the forest, and unusual method of
creation remain intact.

Yellow Mushrooms provide the taming connection through OTAB's interpretation
of Valheim's environmental storytelling surrounding death, decay and the
underground places where these mushrooms grow.

Where Valheim provides explicit information, OTAB follows it.

Where Valheim leaves gaps, OTAB uses limited interpretation to support the
taming system without presenting those additions as established canon.
